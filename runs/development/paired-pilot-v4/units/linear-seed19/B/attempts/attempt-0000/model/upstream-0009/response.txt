Selecting polynomial complexity and regularization from limited data requires distinguishing observed predictive performance from conclusions that depend on an incomplete comparison. We investigate this issue on a fixed regression task with 80 training examples and 64 validation examples, using polynomial ridge regression under unchanged host fitting conventions. The preregistered study compares degrees \(d \in \{1,2,3\}\) and penalties \(\alpha \in \{0.0,0.1,1.0\}\), with selection by minimum validation mean squared error and exact ties resolved by lower degree and then lower penalty. Its central question is whether regularization changes the ranking of polynomial degrees, motivated by the hypothesis that a linear model with weak regularization will perform well on the supplied approximately linear observations. The available evidence covers only \(d=1\), \(\alpha=0.0\): an actual host execution, reported as independently verified, yielded training MSE \(0.004302040179367455\) and validation MSE \(0.00672068755185014\). Although the host reports two actual CPU attempts, it reports only one distinct configuration; these counts provide no comparison with alternative degrees or penalties. The measured linear baseline is consistent with the motivating hypothesis, but does not establish optimality, benefits from regularization, or a degree--regularization interaction. Accordingly, \(d=1\), \(\alpha=0.0\) is the provisional choice among the supplied evaluated configurations, while the remaining eight candidates require host evaluation. This report provides a documented baseline and a fixed selection protocol, rather than a completed comparative study. The validation score describes performance on the supplied selection split; test performance, statistical significance, and independent generalization estimates remain unavailable.
